Background. Most digital computers represent every number with bits, and a bit has exactly two states. The choice is practical: a transistor that is either off or on is easy to build, easy to test and tolerant of noise. Nevertheless, two is not the only possible base. A ternary digit, often called a trit, has three states. In balanced ternary these states stand for minus one, zero and plus one, which has a pleasant property: negative numbers need no separate sign, and rounding a number to its nearest integer is the same as cutting off the digits after the point.

Interest in the idea has returned because of language models. Researchers found that the weight matrices of a transformer can be trained so that every weight is one of minus one, zero or plus one, with a single scale factor per tensor or per small block of weights. Such a model needs roughly one and a half bits per weight instead of sixteen. The multiplication of a weight by an activation then degenerates into an addition, a subtraction or a skip, so the arithmetic of the matrix products becomes cheap. Generating one token reads every weight once, and the speed of that read is limited by the memory system, not by the arithmetic. A compact storage format therefore matters at least as much as the cleverness of the multiplication, and a good implementation tries to decode the packed weights with as few instructions as possible so that the processor can keep up with the memory.

Several packing schemes exist. The simplest stores four trits in a byte, using two bits per trit, which wastes about a quarter of the available information. A denser scheme packs five trits into one byte, because three to the power of five is two hundred and forty three, which still fits into the two hundred and fifty six values of a byte. That reaches one point six bits per weight. The price is a more involved decoding step, and the whole design has to make sure that this step is hidden behind the memory transfers instead of slowing them down.

Finally, correctness deserves attention. When a new kernel replaces an old one, the generated text must stay exactly the same under greedy decoding, otherwise a tiny rounding difference can grow through dozens of layers and change which word the model prefers. For this reason careful projects compare the logits of the new implementation with a trusted reference token by token, and they publish speed measurements only after that comparison has passed.
